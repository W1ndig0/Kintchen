KITCHEN HUB - HISTORY
=====================
The update notes of every version before 11, newest first, in one file. (The notes for 11.x are the
UPDATE_11.*.txt files.)


####################################################################################################
KITCHEN HUB 10.10.5 - THE EDITING SCREEN, THE STANDBY CLOCK, AND OTHER THINGS THAT LOOKED WRONG
Installs by itself like any update: Settings > Updates > CHECK FOR UPDATE.
Things that were visibly wrong, found from photos of the fridge, and the editing screen laid out the way you asked.

WHAT WAS WRONG
 1  THE EDIT BUTTONS WERE INVISIBLE. In 10.10 the whole bottom bar became the sleep timer, so most of
    it is black. The buttons that appear when you tap a tile (S, M, L, XL, MOVE, SHUFFLE...) had black
    edges and black writing, so on the black part of the bar they simply could not be seen; only the
    one chosen size showed. Every button now has its own white face, and the chosen one is black with
    a white edge, so they read on any part of the bar.
 2  A TILE STAYED "BEING EDITED" AFTER YOU PRESSED DONE. The tile you had last tapped kept its
    editing cover for good: on the home page the Calendar was blank with "CHANGE IT IN THE BAR
    BELOW" printed across it. Every tile you tapped after that left another cover stacked behind
    it. They are all removed now, every time.
 3  The "change it in the bar below" note sat on the same line as the tile's name on a wide tile,
    jammed against it. It is on its own line under the name.
 4  The PREV / NEXT buttons said CHEVL and CHEVR (the page flip on the home bar, and the arrows when
    reading a story, a recipe, the day view, the calendar and the horoscope). They say PREV and NEXT.
 5  The title across the top ("GOOD EVENING", "EDITING LAYOUT") lost its last letter. The two empty
    side cells took half the screen, leaving the title only about a quarter of it. The title now has
    the room.

THE EDITING SCREEN, AS YOU ASKED
 6  Tap a tile and its own controls are ON the tile: its size (S M L XL), the two arrows that move it
    along, and the SHUFFLE switch. They are big enough for a finger, and the type shrinks on a small
    tile until they all fit. The bottom bar keeps only what doesn't belong on a tile: TO PAGE 2 / 1,
    HIDE, and ADD / OPTIONS. Tapping a button on the tile no longer also deselects the tile.
 7  The bottom bar is plain white while you edit (the black sleep-timer fill is off; the countdown to
    standby is still in the left rail).
 8  On the home bar the page flip says PAGE 2 or PAGE 1.

THE STANDBY CLOCK WAS SOMETIMES WRONG
 9  The Kindle can wake a second or two before the time it was asked to wake at, and "Hour only" asks
    for the wake 3 seconds early on purpose. The hourly redraw then drew the standby page with the OLD
    hour, so an hour-only clock stayed an hour behind until the next redraw, and a minute clock a
    minute behind until its next tick. A wake now waits out those last seconds first, and so does a
    minute tick that comes early.

THE STANDBY PAGE (ASLEEP) LAYOUT
10  On the upright screen the title was cut to "THE KITCHEN H" (it is shrunk to fit now).
11  The weather line ("Overcast - H 0 L -6") wrapped under the weather picture on the upright screen;
    it stays beside the picture now.
12  The spot where the Kindle draws the clock digits and the weather animation was read in the middle
    of drawing the page, before the weather picture, the bird of the hour and the last fitting of the
    column. On a crowded page the digits and the animation landed a few pixels off the picture
    (over the text beside it). They are read after the page is finished, and again if the bird's
    picture arrives late and the column is fitted again. The bird's picture arriving late also pushed
    the horoscope off the bottom of the column; that is fixed too.

UNDER THE BONNET
13  t26 draws the editing screen in the same old browser the Kindle uses and checks the above (it
    fails on 10.10.4 on fourteen counts). t27 simulates the early wake: the old loop starts every
    redraw 3 seconds before its time, this one starts it on time. t28 draws the standby page at all four
    rotations, plain and crowded, and checks the title, the weather line, overlaps, the column, and
    that the loop is told the true places (it fails on 10.10.4 on twenty-two counts).

KITCHEN HUB 10.10.4 - FASTER TAPS, AND THREE MORE FAULTS
Installs by itself like any update: Settings > Updates > CHECK FOR UPDATE.
No new features. 10.10.3 was a hunt for faults; this one measures the hub instead of guessing, and
makes the part you actually wait for - a tap in Settings - about three times quicker.

WHY SETTINGS FELT SLOW
 1  While the background loop is dealing with one tap it cannot hear the next one. So however long the
    loop takes to handle a tap is exactly how long the screen feels stuck. Measured here, almost all
    of that time was the loop looking through the whole settings message about forty times over,
    starting a separate little program for each look, even for the thirty-odd settings your tap had
    nothing to do with. It now checks once whether your tap mentions a group of settings at all and
    skips the whole group if it does not. A tap went from 43 to 15 milliseconds on this machine; the
    Kindle is some ten to twenty times slower than that, so it is the same saving in the part you feel.
 2  The clock, the "wakes at" times and the file the loop hands the page are now worked out without
    starting a program each time either - the same answers, fewer moving parts.
 3  When the loop does turn a tap away because it is still busy, the page now tries again after a sixth
    of a second instead of waiting the better part of a second, and staggers repeat tries slightly so
    two taps sent together stop colliding.

FAULTS FOUND AND FIXED
 4  THE ALMANAC NEVER HAD ANY RELEASES IN IT. The cloud looks up the week's big games, films and TV
    every six hours, and then the Sunday summary ("last 7 days: spent ...") was written over the top of
    it before it was sent out. So the fridge's Almanac has always said "No big game, film or TV
    releases this week". Both now have their own place and both arrive.
 5  Once all your books were locked with your passphrase, the cloud still downloaded every book in full
    every quarter of an hour to check - every part of every book, for nothing. It now remembers which
    books it has already locked.
 6  A bird whose photo could not be looked up (Wikipedia down, or no licence it was allowed to use) was
    given up on for a whole month. It tries again after three days.

UNDER THE BONNET
 7  A new test times the loop with every group of settings mentioned against a plain tap and fails if
    the difference has crept back. It fails on 10.10.3 and passes on this one, so the saving cannot be
    lost again without something going red.
 8  The build check now refuses to start if a setting is ever handled inside a group that does not name
    it - the mistake that would otherwise make a setting silently stop working.
 9  Proof that none of the above changed any behaviour: thirty-nine settings were each sent to the old
    loop and the new one, and everything both of them wrote was compared byte for byte.

KITCHEN HUB 10.10.3 - A NIGHT SPENT GOING THROUGH EVERYTHING
Installs by itself like any update: Settings > Updates > CHECK FOR UPDATE.
No new features: this is a pass over the whole hub looking for faults, and what it found.

THE BIG ONE
 1  A WEEK WITH NO SHIFTS FROZE THE HUB. The page tells the background loop when you are at work, and
    when there is nothing in the next seven days it says "no work times at all". The loop wrote that
    down in a way that left the file it hands the page (state.js) broken. From then on the page could
    not read anything from the loop: the countdown stopped, it never learned it was asleep or awake,
    and taps looked dead. Worst of all it was silent - a file that will not load still counts as
    loaded, so nothing appeared in the log. Fixed, and the page now says so in the log if a file from
    the loop ever stops making sense again.

OTHER FAULTS FOUND AND FIXED
 2  After the Kindle refused to go to sleep once (it happens when the screen or Wi-Fi is still busy),
    the hub thought the battery was at 3%, quietly switched to the 15-minute clock and stopped the
    weather animation for the rest of the night.
 3  MAKE NEW KEYS also turned the Kindle on its side: making keys and rotating the screen were asking
    for the same thing by mistake.
 4  If a small timing file was cut off in the middle of being written (a flat battery, a reset), the
    next sync stopped dead at that point: no weather, no news, or a half-finished list sync.
 5  Tapping SYNC in the last seconds before the Kindle nodded off started a sync that the Kindle then
    cut off by sleeping, and could leave Wi-Fi on. Now anything you ask for in those seconds keeps it
    awake, not just a tap on the screen.
 6  The QR button on the shopping list always showed the old picture-of-the-list code instead of the
    live list link for a paired phone.
 7  The battery report's "days left" was worked out from how much history it had, not from the battery.
    The first day's report also had no starting percentage.
 8  Everything the page does on a timer came due at the same moment the Kindle woke up and ran in one
    lump - part of why it felt slow for a second after waking. They are spread out again now.
 9  The battery report now shows in Settings > Sleep the first time you open it, not the second.
10  Smaller things: a story cut off in the middle of an accented character no longer leaves a black
    diamond in the news; a price that isn't a number can't stop the weather and news loading; the file
    with your passphrase keeps its private permissions if it ever needs repairing; the hub clears its
    own leftover listener instead of refusing to start; the weather animation keeps to its ten seconds
    on a slow screen.

UNDER THE BONNET
11  The list code exists in four places (the Kindle page, the phone, the cloud and the fridge's own
    shell). They are now checked against each other automatically, including a four-hundred-line test
    that the shell's merge gives exactly the same answer as the phone's.
12  If anything in the page's state handling ever throws, it is logged once and the page carries on,
    instead of the screen quietly stopping.

KITCHEN HUB 10.10.2 - THE NIGHT CLOCK
Installs by itself like any update: Settings > Updates > CHECK FOR UPDATE.

FIX
 1  At night the standby page switches to the big night screen. 10.10 only knew where the clock was on the
    day page, so at night the page left its "10:--" placeholder up, and the loop drew the minutes where the
    day page's digits had been (the small "31" above the hour). The night screen now draws its clock from
    the same pictures as the loop, in a new extra-large size, and tells the loop exactly where the digits
    are. If a standby page has no clock, the loop draws none.
 2  The first minute tick after the Kindle goes to sleep now redraws every digit, not just the ones that
    changed, so a clock that was out of date on screen is corrected within a minute.
 3  The standby page is drawn again on each hourly redraw (and when a minute tick crosses the hour). Before,
    only the clock was updated, so the "wakes ..." line, the story, the bird of the hour and the switch
    between the day page and the night screen could stay out of date.
 4  The bottom bar no longer says "press the button" twice while asleep.

KITCHEN HUB 10.10.1 - FIXES THE FREEZING
Installs by itself like any update: Settings > Updates > CHECK FOR UPDATE.

FIX
 1  THE CAUSE of the freezing in 10.10: when the minute changed, the left rail redrew its clock and lost the
    status (awake, battery, updated) with it. 10.10 had dropped the check that used to put it back, so from
    then on every refresh of the bottom bar failed: the sleep timer stopped moving, every tap caused an
    error, and Settings stopped updating. The rail now keeps the status, and if it is ever lost the next
    refresh puts it back. A refresh that fails can no longer stop anything else. Reproduced in an old
    browser like the Kindle's and confirmed fixed.
 2  A tap on the sleeping screen opened whatever it landed on (Settings, the calendar...) while the hub went
    on sleeping, so the Kindle seemed frozen with that screen up. Now a tap while asleep only wakes the hub.
    The power button works as before.
 3  The bottom bar was rebuilt about a second after every tap (the loop answers a tap with a new state),
    so the next tap on its buttons often did nothing. It is now only rebuilt when something on it changes.
 4  Any message from the page during the hourly redraw, or in the seconds before standby, switched the page
    back to "awake". It now stays on the standby view.
 5  Where the clock digits and the weather picture are on the standby page was sent while the loop wasn't
    listening, so it could get lost. The loop now listens in those seconds, and a tap then keeps the hub
    awake. The page also tries again later if a send didn't arrive.
 6  Two messages sent together kept retrying at the same moment and colliding. Retries are now spread out.

KITCHEN HUB 10.10 - QUICK, QUIET AND EASY ON THE BATTERY
Installs by itself like any update: Settings > Updates > CHECK FOR UPDATE.

SYNC
 1  The fridge only goes online when you tap SYNC. Waking it with the button or a tap no longer syncs, and
    nothing on the page fetches anything by itself. Everything from the cloud (weather, news, markets,
    birds, deals) arrives together in one package.
 2  An edit on the fridge (a list item ticked, added or removed) goes up right away in a short burst: Wi-Fi
    on for 20 s at most, lists only. Settings > Sleep > "Send fridge edits" can hold them for the next
    SYNC instead.
 3  Daily session (3 am by default): one quiet trip online that checks the clock, brings in phone, Siri
    and PC changes and commands, fetches the cloud package only if it changed, checks for updates, and
    sends the log and battery report. Phone list changes reach the fridge here, or when you tap SYNC.
 4  The cloud brain does its heavy work every 2 hours and repackages only when something really changed.
    It stays far inside GitHub's free limits.

STANDBY
 5  The clock keeps ticking every minute all night while the Kindle sleeps. Only the changed digits are
    drawn, from ready-made pictures, with no page or Wi-Fi involved, so each tick costs a second or so.
    Settings > Sleep: every minute, every 15 min, hour only or off; a different choice at night.
    This replaces the old home / work / night wake intervals (the night hours are still used).
 6  Every hour the standby page is redrawn cleanly: the weather picture plays a ~10 s animation of the
    current weather, then stays still, and the bird of the hour and the news change.
 7  The battery report: each morning the log, the phone (Fridge tab) and Settings > Sleep show what
    yesterday cost: battery used, wakes, time awake, Wi-Fi trips and seconds, clock ticks.
 8  Battery saver (on by default): below 15% the clock changes every 15 minutes and the animation is skipped.

SCREEN
 9  The left rail always shows awake, battery and last updated. APPS moved to the bottom of the rail; the
    HIDE button is gone (the rail tucks away by itself while asleep).
10  The whole bottom bar is the sleep timer: it fills up as the Kindle gets ready to sleep, with the text
    inverted over the filled part. The bottom bar is now only for app buttons and editing, and its
    buttons are 50% bigger.
11  Editing: tap a tile to select it; its own buttons appear at the bottom (size, move, to page, shuffle,
    hide, options). No more hunting for small handles.
12  The page is quicker: one shared timer instead of a dozen, data re-read only after a sync, and the
    data shown only redrawn when it changed.

KITCHEN HUB 10.9.4 - CLEANUP AFTER THE FIRST REAL START
Installs by itself like any update: Settings > Updates > CHECK FOR UPDATE.

FIX
 1  The start-up error box stayed on the screen after the hub had started. It now clears itself as soon as
    the hub is running, and only shows up at all if the hub can't start.
 2  The box (and the log) listed "a file failed to load: horo.js". That file is optional (the hub makes its
    own horoscopes when it's missing); such files are now noted once in the log, never on the screen.
 3  "JavaScript execution exceeded timeout": the 10.9.2 start-up black box reported every step on the spot
    and made the page wait for each one, so start-up took about 19 s instead of ~10 and the Kindle's
    browser cut a script short. Steps are now only remembered; errors are still reported.
 4  data.js (weather and news) wouldn't load on the Kindle: "Parse error" on its news line. Invisible
    line-separator and control characters from news text (fine for the cloud's newer JavaScript, not for
    the Kindle's older engine) are now cleaned out in the cloud and on the Kindle. If data.js ever fails
    again, the log describes the failing line, so the cause can be pinned down.

FROM 10.9.3
 5  THE CAUSE of every 10.9 rollback (blank screen): while the page started, a 10.9 step that re-sends
    list items the Kindle file was missing wrote a note through a helper whose memory was only set up
    ~1900 lines further down the page. On a Kindle with saved list items that crashed the page's
    start-up, so it never said "I'm up". The 10.9.2 black box caught the exact error. The helper is set
    up first now, and a list hiccup can no longer stop start-up. Reproduced and confirmed fixed in an old
    browser like the Kindle's, with list items of every kind.
 6  GitHub's upload check now also scans the page for start-up code that reads something before it is
    set. It flags 10.9.2 and passes 10.9.3, so this kind of bug can't reach the fridge again.
 7  The weather in data.js could be a cut-off download (it only had to contain the words "hourly" and
    "daily"), which made data.js fail to load: no weather or news. The cloud now leaves out any file that
    isn't valid, and the Kindle only uses a whole weather reply.
 8  10.9 turned the frontlight OFF every time it started or woke (the new light setting defaulted to
    Off). Now the hub leaves the light alone unless you pick a level in Settings > Sleep > Light while
    you use it ("Kindle's own" is the default).

SECURITY
 9  Authenticator codes: Settings > Security > SET UP shows a code for Google or Microsoft Authenticator.
    After that, pairing codes, the first cloud connection and new keys need a fresh 6-digit code.
10  Pairing codes are sealed: the QR holds everything locked with a one-time code shown next to it, never
    inside it. A copied or photographed link alone opens nothing. The shopping-list QR only opens the app.
11  MAKE NEW KEYS: the Kindle makes a new passphrase, alert channel and monitor channel itself. Old
    lists and books still open until you tap FINISH; the screen walks you through the GitHub secrets.
    Fridges that used the old built-in passphrase are told to do this.
12  A new GitHub token goes in on the phone (Fridge > New GitHub token); the fridge picks it up itself.
13  The fridge's settings port needs a key now, so other devices on your Wi-Fi can't stop the hub, undo an
    update or send phone alerts. Fixed: a phone alert could be made to send a file off the Kindle.
14  The phone app and TV page are hidden from search engines and do nothing until paired. The PC helper
    asks for the pairing link (it no longer stays in PowerShell's history) and locks its saved token.

BATTERY
15  Timer wakes are quick: the fridge refreshes the asleep screen in a few seconds and sleeps again,
    instead of staying awake for 2 minutes with nobody there (and flashing the screen twice). The power
    button, or a tap while it refreshes, opens the hub as before. Settings > Sleep > Timer wakes.
16  The asleep clock is honest: the exact time and "until 6:00 PM" (at night, "back at"). Or choose
    "every minute": a 3-second wake each minute changes just the time (no Wi-Fi, no touch).
17  Settings > Sleep shows how much battery an hour it uses lately, to compare settings. New: a light
    setting for while you use the hub (off asleep), or leave it to the Kindle (the default).

HOME SCREEN
18  Spare room goes to tiles whose content didn't fit; an empty shopping list or idle timer shrinks.
19  Small cards fill leftover space: bird of the hour, tomorrow's shift, sunrise or sunset, the next
    holiday or event. No more blank holes.
20  Live boosts: a running timer, rain or a storm in the next 3 hours, or a shift within 2 hours makes
    that tile bigger (up to two, kept at least 30 minutes so the screen doesn't jump).
21  Bird of the hour: birds of Prince George for the season, with real photos (free-licence Wikimedia
    Commons pictures, photographer credited).

KITCHEN MONITOR
22  APPS > Kitchen screen controls the TV page on a mini PC: dashboard, big clock, your timer, a tavern
    menu board, the week, news, screen off, cook a recipe step by step. Pair the monitor once:
    Settings > Security > TV / MONITOR. Commands are locked and can't be replayed.
23  See-through look for transparent screens: black is clear glass, the type is warm and bright.

SYSTEM
24  Settings > System (was Log): a task manager - CPU, memory, storage, battery, the busiest programs and
    the Kindle's own services. It only looks; the list goes to your cloud log so a later "hub mode" can
    pause only services that are safe to pause on your Kindle.

FIXES
25  Changes made on the fridge could get lost when its background loop was busy; they're sent one batch
    at a time now, retried, and sent again if the fridge's file doesn't have them.
26  The cloud brain can't hang any more when GitHub or ntfy doesn't answer (it stops after 45 s).
27  The asleep front page no longer cuts a story in the middle of a symbol.

A bug-fix release. No new features: everything here makes the hub start, restart, update and stop
without locking up the Kindle.

INSTALL
 Because the fridge may still be running an older mix of files, copy this version over USB once:
 1  Tap "Kitchen Hub Reset" in the Kindle library (or restart the Kindle), then plug it into the PC.
 2  Copy everything in kindle/shiftcal over extensions/shiftcal on the Kindle. Your cloud.conf, lists
    and settings are not in the kit, so they are kept. (eco.conf in the kit holds the defaults; skip it
    if you changed settings by hand.)
 3  Copy the five files in kindle/documents to the Kindle's documents folder (the library buttons).
 4  Unplug, tap "Kitchen Hub Display". From 10.5 on, updates arrive by themselves again.
 GitHub: upload the whole kit as before. The build check now also refuses an upload where the page and
 the scripts are different versions, a script has Windows line endings, or a needed file is missing.

"THE APPLICATION COULD NOT BE STARTED"
 1  The hub page was started twice: the launcher opened it, and the background loop sent a second
    "start" a few seconds later, while the page was still loading - and again on every wake. The page is
    now started in exactly one place, and never while it is already running.
 2  If the Kindle was slow to open the page, the launcher started a second copy of it behind the Kindle's
    back after 10 seconds. That is gone; the launcher waits up to 30 seconds for the one copy.
 3  Two taps on Kitchen Hub Display started two launchers that fought each other. The second one now
    steps aside.
 4  The hub recognises its own page by the exact process it started, so the Kindle's browser (or another
    app) is never mistaken for the hub, and the hub never closes another app's window.

THE RESTART LOOP
 5  "Install update" when there was nothing new still said "update installed - restarting the hub". A
    second tap on INSTALL restarted the hub over and over. Now only a real install restarts it, once.
 6  A tap the page re-sends right after a restart is recognised and not run a second time.
 7  If the hub page closes on its own 3 times in 15 minutes, the hub stops cleanly (and says why on the
    next launch) instead of reopening it forever.

STOPPING AND CLEAN-UP
 8  One stop script (stop_hub.sh) for everything: the launcher, Reset, Soft Reset, Kill Background and
    the hub's own exit all use it. Before 10.5 five scripts each had their own list of what to stop.
 9  Stopping asks nicely first and only forces programs that don't listen. After a forced stop it puts
    back what the hub changes while it runs (the paused power manager, the wake alarm, the CPU setting).
10  A sync that runs too long is stopped together with everything it started, and Wi-Fi goes off.
    Before, its downloads kept running and Wi-Fi could stay on through the night.
11  A page that never talks to the hub is stopped after 90 seconds as before - and the page now says
    "I'm up" every 10 seconds until the hub hears it, so a working page is no longer stopped by mistake.
12  Scripts saved on Windows (CR LF line endings) are fixed during the launch instead of stopping it.

UPDATES
13  Updates are checked on every download (certificate check), and so are your lists and books. The
    kit carries its own up-to-date list of trusted certificates (cacert.pem). Only weather, news and
    pictures may still be fetched unchecked, and only if a checked connection is impossible.
14  If moving a new file into place fails halfway, the files already moved are put back.
15  New default: "Tell me" - the fridge checks each night and shows "update ready"; you tap to install.
    (Settings > Phone > Updates still offers "At night".)

LISTS, PHONE AND PC
16  A Siri or PC note picked up twice no longer re-opens items you had already ticked off.
17  Your GitHub token no longer shows in the Kindle's process list (or in Reset's diag.txt).
18  Two list edits at the same moment can no longer overwrite each other (the lock is only broken
    when the program that held it is gone).
19  A backslash typed in an item stays a backslash.
20  PC: "kh add milk, eggs" adds two items. "kh done milk" ticks Milk but not Buttermilk; when more than
    one item matches, it lists them and asks for the full name.
21  Locked items get their random part from the system's secure random source.

DATA
22  When the weather or every news feed fails, the last forecast and stories are kept instead of an
    empty tile. A slow network can't stretch a sync past 4 minutes: the extras wait for the next one.
23  A news.conf saved on Windows works; a source name with quotes no longer breaks the news.
24  On This Day: an event with quotes in its text no longer blanks the whole data file.
25  Horoscopes: when only a few signs arrive, the next sync tries again instead of waiting a day.
26  Photos over 3 MB are skipped, photos in use are never trimmed, and web pages over 3 MB are refused.
27  Pantry: ground coffee, ground cinnamon, chicken broth, fish sauce, black pepper, pearl barley,
